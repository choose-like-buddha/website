The laptop comes to dinner. Messages follow you into bed, and weekends blur into catch-up days. You tell yourself it's just this season, just this deadline, but the season never ends. Meanwhile the people you love get the tired leftovers of you, and the hobbies that once made you feel alive gather dust. You're present nowhere: distracted at home and guilty at work, running on a treadmill that speeds up whenever you almost catch your breath.

## The Mindful Approach

The Buddha taught the Middle Way, a path between indulgence and harsh deprivation. Balance works the same way. It isn't a fixed ratio of hours but a quality of attention, knowing where you are and being fully there. When work swallows life, the answer is rarely a dramatic escape. It's a series of small, deliberate choices that return you to what matters, repeated until they become the shape of your days.

- **Draw one clear line.** Boundaries that are vague get eroded by the urgent. Choose a single, concrete limit you can actually keep, such as no email after eight or a protected Saturday morning. Hold it gently but firmly. One boundary honored does more than ten promised and quietly broken under pressure.
- **Be wholly where you are.** Half-attention is exhausting because you pay twice, once at work and again at home. When you're with your family, put the phone in another room. When you're working, work. Presence turns ordinary hours into real rest, and it lets you give fully instead of spreading yourself thin.
- **Question the story of indispensability.** Much of our overwork is fueled by the belief that everything will collapse without us. Usually it won't. Notice the fear underneath the constant availability, the worry about being seen as less committed. Seeing that fear clearly loosens its grip and lets you choose rather than react.

## A Practice for Today

Tonight, pick a moment to close your work completely. Shut the laptop, silence notifications, and say quietly to yourself, "The workday is done." Then spend fifteen minutes doing something that belongs only to your life: a walk, a real conversation, a page of a book. Notice any restlessness that pulls you back toward the screen, and let it pass like weather. Your life is not the space left over after work. It deserves your full presence, too.
