It started as a simple comment, a reasonable point. Then came the reply, and your reply to that, and now you're forty messages deep, heart pounding, thumbs flying. Each response feels more urgent than the last. You refresh the thread obsessively, craft the perfect rebuttal, feel the sting when strangers pile on the other side. Dinner goes cold. Sleep gets pushed back. Somewhere along the way the topic stopped mattering — now it's about not letting them have the last word.

## The Mindful Approach

The Buddha taught that holding onto anger is like grasping a hot coal intending to throw it at someone else — you are the one who gets burned. Online arguments amplify this: no tone, no faces, and an audience that rewards heat over understanding. Rarely does anyone change their mind in a comment thread. Wisdom lies in recognizing when a conversation has turned into combat, and choosing to set down the coal.

- **Notice the body before the keyboard.** Racing pulse, clenched jaw, shallow breath — these are signals that you're no longer discussing but defending. When you feel them, stop typing. Nothing written from that state is likely to bring clarity, and it will almost certainly bring more fire back your way.
- **Ask what you actually want.** Do you want to be understood, to learn something, or simply to win? If it's understanding, a private message or a single calm clarification may serve better. If it's winning, recognize that the victory you're chasing is imaginary — and the cost to your peace is very real.
- **Let the last word go.** Walking away can feel like conceding, but there's great strength in leaving a thread unfinished. The other person may declare triumph; let them. Your wellbeing isn't measured in replies. Choosing silence over escalation is a quiet act of self-respect.

## A Practice for Today

The next time you feel an online exchange heating up, write your reply — but don't send it. Step away from the screen for ten minutes. Walk, drink some water, breathe deeply into your belly. When you return, reread both the thread and your draft with fresh eyes. Ask: "Will sending this bring more understanding or more heat?" More often than not, you'll choose to delete it. Every argument you decline is a little more peace you get to keep.
