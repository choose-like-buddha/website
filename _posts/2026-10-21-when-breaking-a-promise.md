You said you'd be there, and you weren't. You committed to finishing it, and you didn't. Maybe life got in the way, maybe you overpromised, maybe you simply forgot. Now there's a knot of guilt in your stomach and a conversation you'd rather avoid. You imagine their disappointment, rehearse excuses, and wonder whether they'll ever quite trust you the same way again. The weight of your own unkept words sits with you all day.

## The Mindful Approach

Everyone breaks promises sometimes. What matters most is not flawless consistency but how you respond when you fall short. Buddhist teaching emphasizes right speech — words that are truthful and made with care — and this includes the courage to own our failures. Guilt can be useful when it moves you toward repair; it becomes harmful when it turns into hiding, defensiveness, or endless self-punishment.

- **Take responsibility without excuses.** It's tempting to explain away what happened or minimize its impact. Resist that urge. A simple, sincere acknowledgment — "I told you I would, I didn't, and I'm sorry" — carries more healing power than any elaborate justification. Honesty shows respect for the person you let down.
- **Make amends where you can.** Ask how you might set things right, or offer a concrete way to make up for what was missed. Then follow through carefully. Repair isn't about erasing the lapse; it's about demonstrating, through action, that their trust still matters to you and that you intend to earn it back.
- **Learn to promise more wisely.** Reflect on why the promise broke. Did you say yes too quickly, to avoid disappointing someone? Were you overcommitted? Let this shape how you give your word in the future. Fewer promises, made thoughtfully and kept faithfully, build far more trust than many made carelessly.

## A Practice for Today

If you've broken a promise recently, reach out today with a clear, honest acknowledgment — no excuses, no drawn-out self-criticism, just ownership and an offer to repair. Then take a quiet moment for yourself and place a hand over your heart. Say inwardly: "I fell short, and I am learning." Before making your next commitment, pause for one breath and ask whether you can truly keep it. Integrity is not never failing; it's how faithfully you return to your word.
