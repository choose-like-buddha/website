The test results haven't come. The email you're hoping for hasn't arrived. You're stuck in a queue, a waiting room, a season of life where the next step depends on someone else's timeline. Each hour stretches like taffy. You check your phone compulsively, run scenarios in your head, and feel your life suspended in a kind of limbo. It's as though everything real is happening somewhere ahead of you, and you're trapped in the hallway outside.

## The Mindful Approach

Waiting feels unbearable largely because we treat it as empty time — a gap to be endured before life resumes. But there is no pause button on your existence. These hours are as much your life as any others. When you stop straining toward the future and settle into the present, waiting transforms from a prison into a place. The outcome may still be uncertain, but your experience of the in-between can change completely.

- **Notice the leaning forward.** Impatience is a physical posture of the mind, always tilting toward what's next. Catch yourself doing it. Feel the restlessness in your legs, the tightness behind your eyes. Simply noticing this strain loosens it a little, bringing you back from the imagined future into the room you're actually sitting in.
- **Stop feeding the loop of speculation.** Replaying possible outcomes feels productive, but it mostly multiplies your anxiety without changing anything. When you catch yourself spinning, gently label it "planning" or "worrying," and return to something concrete — your breath, the sounds around you, the task in front of you.
- **Fill the time with presence, not distraction.** Endless scrolling makes waiting feel longer and hollower. Instead, use the time with intention: read something nourishing, call a friend, take a walk, tend to something small. Living fully now makes the wait lighter and leaves you steadier for whatever comes.

## A Practice for Today

Wherever you're waiting — in a line, in a lobby, in a longer season of uncertainty — try one minute of mindful waiting. Set your phone face down. Feel your feet on the floor and your weight in the chair. Listen to the layered sounds around you: voices, footsteps, the hum of machines. When your mind reaches for the outcome, softly return. You may find the minute passes more gently than any before it. The future will arrive in its own time; this moment is already here.
