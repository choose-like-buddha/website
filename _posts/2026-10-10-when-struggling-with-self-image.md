You catch your reflection in a shop window and wince. The photos from last weekend make you want to hide. You compare your body, your face, your life to carefully filtered images and always come up short. The inner commentary is relentless: too heavy, too plain, too much, not enough. You'd never speak to a friend this way, yet the criticism flows so easily toward yourself that it feels like simple truth rather than the cruelty it really is.

## The Mindful Approach

How we see ourselves is shaped by conditioning, comparison, and culture, not by some objective measure of worth. Mindfulness teaches us to observe our thoughts without automatically believing them. The Buddha also taught impermanence: every body changes, ages, and eventually returns to the earth. Seen this way, clinging to an ideal appearance is a battle no one can win, and releasing it opens space for gratitude toward the body that carries you.

- **Hear the critic as a voice, not the truth.** When harsh judgments arise, label them: "There's that criticizing thought again." This small step creates distance. You don't have to argue or suppress it, just recognize that it's a mental habit, often learned long ago, rather than an accurate description of who you are today.
- **Shift from appearance to appreciation.** Instead of assessing how your body looks, notice what it does. It breathes without being asked, carries you through the day, lets you hug someone you love. Gratitude for function softens the obsession with form and reconnects you with the living, feeling being behind the reflection you keep scrutinizing.
- **Curate what you consume.** Much of our dissatisfaction is fed by endless images designed to make us feel lacking. Notice which accounts, shows, or conversations leave you feeling worse about yourself. You're allowed to step away. Protecting your mind is an act of self-respect, not avoidance, and it gives kinder perceptions room to grow.

## A Practice for Today

Stand in front of a mirror for one minute. Instead of evaluating, look softly, as you might look at someone you care about. Let your gaze rest on one part of yourself you often criticize, and silently say, "Thank you for carrying me." If judgment appears, notice it and return to gratitude. It may feel strange or even uncomfortable, and that's okay. You're practicing a new way of seeing. The person in the mirror has been waiting a long time for your kindness.
