A new hire arrives with skills you don't have. Your friend group seems fine when you miss a gathering. Headlines warn that machines will soon do your job. A cold thought settles in: anyone could do what I do, and no one would notice if I were gone. You start working harder to prove your necessity, clinging to tasks, bracing for the day someone decides they no longer need you around. The fear hums quietly beneath everything.

## The Mindful Approach

It's true that roles can be filled by others; that's how organizations and communities survive. But a role is not a person. Buddhist thought reminds us that each being arises from a unique web of causes and conditions that can never be duplicated. Your particular way of seeing, caring, and connecting is irreplaceable. The work may be transferable, yet the presence you bring to it belongs to you alone.

- **Separate function from essence.** It helps to acknowledge honestly that some of what you do could be done by others. That's not a verdict on your value; it's simply how tasks work. Your essence, the warmth, humor, and perspective you carry, doesn't fit in a job description and can't be handed off to a successor.
- **Release the need to be indispensable.** Striving to be essential often breeds anxiety, hoarding, and burnout. Ironically, people who share knowledge freely and help others grow tend to be valued more deeply. Let go of guarding your territory. Contributing generously is a more peaceful path than defending your spot against every perceived threat.
- **Invest in what's uniquely yours.** Rather than competing on sameness, lean into what makes your contribution distinct: your relationships, your judgment, your particular blend of experiences. Cultivate those. No one else has walked your path, and the insights it has given you are yours to offer the world, again and again.

## A Practice for Today

Write down three qualities you bring to your relationships or work that have nothing to do with specific skills: perhaps patience, a way of making people laugh, or a knack for noticing what's unsaid. Read the list slowly and let it land. Then offer one of those qualities intentionally today, without needing anyone to thank you for it. Notice how it feels to give from what you are rather than what you do. There has only ever been one you.
