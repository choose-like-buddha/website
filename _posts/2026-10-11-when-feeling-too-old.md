Everyone around you seems younger, faster, more current. You want to learn something new, change careers, or start again, but a voice whispers that the window has closed. You tally the years already gone and feel the weight of the ones remaining. Milestones you expected to reach by now are still out of sight. The world appears built for the young, and you're left wondering whether your moment has quietly passed while you weren't paying attention.

## The Mindful Approach

Aging is one of the great truths the Buddha contemplated, and he didn't treat it as a tragedy but as a teacher. Impermanence means every age is a passing moment, not a fixed sentence. The feeling of being too old is usually built on comparison and outdated timelines, not reality. What you have now, experience, perspective, and hard-won patience, is a kind of wealth that simply wasn't available to your younger self.

- **Question the imaginary deadline.** Where did your idea of "too late" come from? Often it's a script absorbed from culture or family, not a law of nature. People begin painting, studying, falling in love, and building businesses at every stage of life. Examine the timeline you're measuring yourself against, and ask whether it deserves your belief.
- **Honor what the years have given you.** Age brings things youth can't buy: a clearer sense of what matters, resilience from surviving hard seasons, empathy shaped by loss. These are not consolation prizes; they're genuine strengths. Rather than mourning lost time, recognize the depth you've gained and let it inform whatever you choose to do next.
- **Begin with a beginner's mind.** Zen teacher Shunryu Suzuki said that in the beginner's mind there are many possibilities. You can approach something new with curiosity instead of embarrassment. Being a novice at any age is an act of courage and aliveness. Let yourself be clumsy, ask questions, and enjoy the freshness of learning.

## A Practice for Today

Think of one thing you've told yourself you're too old to try. Write it down, then beneath it write one tiny first step you could take this week: watching a tutorial, signing up for a class, having a conversation with someone who's done it. Take a slow breath and remind yourself, "This moment is the youngest I will ever be again." Then take that step, however small. Life doesn't stop inviting you forward; you're invited to answer, now.
