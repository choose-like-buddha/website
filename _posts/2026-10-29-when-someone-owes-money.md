You lent it in good faith — to a friend, a relative, a colleague in a tight spot. Weeks turned into months, and the subject never comes up. They post photos from dinners out, buy new things, act as though nothing is pending. You rehearse how to bring it up, then lose your nerve. Every interaction now carries an invisible weight. You feel foolish for trusting, awkward for caring, and increasingly resentful toward someone you actually like — all over a debt no one mentions.

## The Mindful Approach

Buddhist ethics values right speech — words that are truthful, timely, kind, and helpful. Silence around an unpaid debt often feels polite, but it breeds resentment that corrodes the relationship far more than an honest conversation ever would. The skillful path is to speak clearly and compassionately, to protect your own wellbeing, and to decide consciously what this money is worth compared to your peace of mind.

- **Speak up with clarity and warmth.** Avoiding the topic keeps you stewing. Bring it up simply and without accusation: "I wanted to check in about the money I lent you — when do you think you'll be able to repay it?" A direct, kind question gives them a chance to respond and frees you from endless inner rehearsal.
- **Separate the person from the debt.** They may be struggling, embarrassed, or simply disorganized rather than malicious. Holding space for that possibility allows you to address the issue without condemning their character. You can be firm about repayment while still honoring the relationship and the good in them.
- **Decide what you're willing to release.** Sometimes the money won't come back. Ask yourself honestly: is continued resentment worth the amount? You might set a clear deadline, propose a payment plan, or consciously choose to let it go as a gift. Whatever you decide, decide it — lingering uncertainty is its own debt.

## A Practice for Today

Sit quietly for a few minutes and reflect on what this debt is truly costing you — not in dollars, but in peace, trust, and energy. Then choose one clear step: draft a calm, friendly message asking about repayment, propose a simple plan, or consciously decide to forgive the amount and mean it. Send or commit to your choice before the day ends. However this unfolds, you're choosing honesty over silent resentment. Your peace is worth far more than any sum.
