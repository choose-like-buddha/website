You post something and immediately start watching. Five likes. Twelve. Why is this one doing worse than last week's? You check who viewed it, who didn't, and feel a small drop in your stomach when the numbers stall. You catch yourself shaping moments for the camera, choosing captions to please an algorithm, comparing your follower count to people you've never met. What once felt like sharing now feels like performing — and the audience's approval has quietly become the measure of your day.

## The Mindful Approach

Buddhist wisdom speaks of the "eight worldly winds" — praise and blame, fame and disrepute, gain and loss, pleasure and pain. They blow constantly, and chasing the pleasant ones keeps us forever off balance. Likes and followers are simply a modern version of these winds. Wanting to be seen is deeply human, but when approval becomes your anchor, you're tethered to something that shifts with every scroll.

- **Name the wind when it blows.** When a post performs well and you feel a rush, or poorly and you feel a slump, gently label it: "praise" or "neglect." Seeing these reactions as passing weather, rather than verdicts on your value, helps you stand steadier through both highs and lows.
- **Reconnect with your original intention.** Before posting, ask yourself why you're sharing. Is it to connect, to express, to offer something useful? Or to be validated? Content that comes from genuine intention tends to feel better to create — and its meaning doesn't evaporate when the numbers disappoint.
- **Invest in depth over reach.** A handful of real relationships nourishes far more than thousands of passive followers. Spend some of the energy you'd put into growing an audience into a heartfelt message, a phone call, or time with someone in person. Being truly known beats being widely seen.

## A Practice for Today

Share something today — a photo, a thought, a small creation — and then deliberately don't check its performance for twenty-four hours. Turn off like notifications if you can. Whenever the urge to peek arises, notice it with curiosity, breathe, and remind yourself why you shared in the first place. When you finally look, observe your reaction without judgment. Your value was never up for a vote. Share from the heart, and let the numbers be whatever they are.
