You notice it in small moments: taking the biggest slice, wanting the raise and then immediately wanting more, eyeing someone else's success with a hunger you'd rather not admit. Even after a win, satisfaction fades within days, replaced by the next thing you need to have. You feel a tightness around what's yours, a reluctance to share, a constant calculation of gain. Part of you is uncomfortable with this grasping — yet another part insists that more is exactly what you need.

## The Mindful Approach

In Buddhism, greed is one of the three poisons, alongside aversion and delusion — not because wanting is evil, but because grasping keeps us restless and never at ease. The mind that always reaches is a mind that can't rest in what already is. Rather than condemning yourself, you can study greed with curiosity, see its empty promise, and gently cultivate its antidote: generosity.

- **Observe greed without shame.** When the grasping arises, notice it as a sensation — a tightening in the chest, a leaning forward, a narrowing of focus. Label it softly: "grasping." Shame only drives it underground. Curious observation lets you see its texture and recognize that it is a passing state, not your true nature.
- **Question the promise of "more."** Greed always claims that the next acquisition will bring lasting satisfaction. Look back honestly: how long did the last one satisfy you? Seeing this pattern clearly — the brief spark, the quick fade, the renewed hunger — gradually loosens greed's authority over your choices.
- **Practice small acts of giving.** Generosity is the natural counterbalance to grasping. Give something away — time, money, credit, a compliment — especially when it feels slightly uncomfortable. Each act of giving loosens the grip of scarcity and reminds you that openness, not accumulation, is where genuine happiness tends to live.

## A Practice for Today

Choose one thing today that you would normally hold onto and give it freely: share a meal, donate to a cause, lend a hand without keeping score, or publicly credit someone else's work. As you give, notice the resistance in your body and breathe gently through it. Afterwards, pay attention to how you feel — lighter, perhaps, or unexpectedly warm. Greed narrows the heart; generosity opens it. Every open hand is a step toward true contentment.
